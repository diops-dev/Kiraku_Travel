JAPAN · HISTORY

Three eras, and the country you are about to visit.

Japan does not unfold in a straight line. It is a succession of eras, some of them founding moments, others simply quiet. We tell only those that truly shaped the country you are about to visit.

平

Heian

794 · 1185
The court of Kyoto

The capital moves to Kyoto. For four centuries, a refined aristocracy invents a great deal of Japanese aesthetics: court literature, the cult of the season, a sense of beauty that has never really left the country.

江

Edo

1603 · 1868
The Tokugawa peace

Two hundred and fifty years of peace imposed by the Tokugawa shoguns, a country closed in on itself, and the rise of an urban, merchant culture: the woodblock print, kabuki theatre, and the great highways such as the Nakasendo, still walked today.

明

Meiji

1868 · 1912
The opening

The country is forced open to Western trade, then modernises at an unprecedented pace: industry, army, Western institutions, all while keeping the emperor as its central figure.
